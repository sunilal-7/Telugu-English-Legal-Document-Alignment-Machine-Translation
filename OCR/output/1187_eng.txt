Public Administration :
State Government-Chief Secretary applying for leave-Chief Minister ordering Additional Chief Secretary to be incharge of Chief Secretary until further orders-Government Notification giving effect to Chief Minister's order after expiry of leave - Confusion as to actual status of two officers resulting in conflict and Government's comments on conduct of one of the officers leading to litigation before Central Administrative Tribunal-Tribunal passing adverse remarks against Chief Secretary-Held, government should have clarified position to avoid conflict which jeopardised proper functioning of State Administration-Adverse remarks expunged-High Officials to exercise power in public interest.
Respondent No. 2 in Civil Appeal No. 9560/95 was Chief Secretary of the appellant State in November 1990. On 8. 11.1990 he applied for leave I and the Chief Minister ordered that respondent No. 1, the Additional Chief Secretary, would be in charge of Chief Secretary until further orders. Though the Chief Secretary on 9.11.1990 had clarified that he would be on casual leave for 9.11.1990 and 10.11.1990, Government Notification dated 11.11.1990 was issued posting respondent No. 1 as Chief Secretary, who claimed to have assumed charge on 12.11.1990. Later, by order dated 28,11.1990 the Government notified the appointment of respondent No. 2 as Chief Secretary.
Meanwhile, an incident led to the controversy about the exact status of respondent No. 1 on 12.11.1990. On that date a lady officer, in the rank of Joint Secretary, made a complaint to the Chief Secretary, against respondent No. 1. Respondent No. 2 as Chief Secretary sent the said complaint the same day to respondent No. 1 addressing him as Additional Chief Secretary, to clarify the position. The latter treating himself to be the Chief Secretary on 12.11.90 did not give clarification to Respondent No. 2. H

However, in view of the distressing facts and circumstances which have given rise to these appeals, we consider it necessary to state briefly the relevant facts and express our views which we consider expedient with the hope that it prevents such situations in future at any level of the administration, much B less at the highest level.
Shri P.C. Mishra was posted as the Chairman of the Assam Board of Revenue at the relevant time. By the orders dated 10/10/1991 and 8/9/1992 of the Department of Personnel (A) in the Government of Assam, the displeasure of the Government was conveyed to Shri P.C. Mishra for C his conduct. At any rate some confusion having arisen soon thereafter on 12/11/1990 on the stand taken by Smt. Sablok, Joint Secretary, Department of Personnel and Shri H.N. Unfortunately, this was not done. Even later the adverse effect of this omission and its contribution to escalation of the controversy. was not realised when the matter proceeded further. It is even more C unfortunate that some of the senior most officers of the Indian Administrative Service at the helm of administration in the State, did not realise the implications of their actions which resulted from a clash of egos and percolated to the lower levels of administration.
D It is incumbent for each occupant of every high office to be constantly aware that the power invested in the high office he holds is meant to be exercised in public interest and only for public good, and that it is not meant to be used for any personal benefit or merely to elevate the personal status of the current holder of that office. Constant awareness of the nature of this power and the purpose for which it is meant would prevent situa- E tions leading to clash of egos and the resultant fall out detrimental to public interest. It appears that this lack of perception in all concerned coupled with the failure of the State Government to issue clear orders, precipitated the situation into the unseemly controversy in the present case which must have adversely affected the State Administration. We do hope this incident serves as a warning for the future. We are constrained to make these observations since they are necessary in the context and there is increase in the frequency of such occurrences every-where.
For the aforesaid reasons, we expunge all the adverse remarks contained in the impugned order of the Tribunal against Shri H.N. Das, C the then Chief Secretary. The Tribunal's order would now remain with this modification. The appeals are disposed of accordingly, in these terms.
